overviewHtml: '<p>The journey begins in Casablanca, Morocco''s modern city on the Atlantic coast, and continues to Rabat, the country''s capital, where you can admire the impressive Hassan Tower and the peaceful Kasbah of the Udayas. From there, the road leads north to Chefchaouen, the famous blue city nestled in the Rif Mountains, a place that feels almost magical. Heading south, you will stop at the Roman ruins of Volubilis, explore the imperial city of Meknes, and arrive in Fes, where narrow streets reveal ancient mosques, schools, and colorful markets.</p>

  <p>Further on, the road crosses the cedar forests of Azrou and the alpine town of Ifrane before opening onto the wide Ziz Valley. Soon, the golden dunes of Merzouga rise in the distance. You will also meet nomadic families and listen to the traditional gnawa music that carries the rhythm of the desert.</p>

The path then leads to Ouarzazate, famous for its ancient kasbahs and film studios. Another highlight is Ait Benhaddou, a UNESCO World Heritage village with its earthen houses, before crossing the High Atlas Mountains and reaching Marrakech. In this lively city, the palaces, gardens, and the busy Jemaa el-Fnaa square capture the energy and culture of Morocco.</p>

notes:
- Morocco is bigger than it looks on the map. The drive between Fes and Merzouga, or between Essaouira and Casablanca, can take between 6 and 9 hours, including breaks. Many tours involve several hours of driving, so be prepared for long but beautiful drives through mountains, valleys, and deserts.
- In larger cities such as Marrakech or Fes, you will often stay in riads, traditional Moroccan guesthouses. In smaller towns or in the desert, accommodations may be simpler, but they are generally charming.
- Our guides generally follow a pre-set program, but traffic, weather, or local events can lead to changes, so it is best to stay flexible. If you would like to spend more time in certain places, such as the desert or <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essaouira" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong>Essaouira</strong></a>, discuss possible adjustments with your guide.
- The weather in Morocco can vary greatly. It can be very hot in Marrakech, cooler in the Atlas Mountains, and cold at night in the desert. Bring clothes you can layer, a warm jacket, and comfortable shoes for walking.

itinerary:
- title: 'Day 1: Arrival in Casablanca'
  html: '<p>Your journey begins on the first day with your arrival in Casablanca, Morocco''s bustling commercial hub on the Atlantic. Depending on your flight''s landing time, you may have time to visit the city. The Hassan II Mosque, standing proudly on the seafront, offers a striking first impression of Moroccan architecture and spirituality.</p>

    <p>Later, you can stroll along the Corniche, where the sea breeze will set the mood for the adventure ahead. You will spend the night in Casablanca, where you can rest before the days of travel to come.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation:</strong><br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Melliber or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Art Palace Suite & Spa or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 2: Casablanca - Rabat - Chefchaouen'
  html: '<p>On the second day, you leave Casablanca and head to Rabat, the country''s political capital. Here, the royal palace, the Hassan Tower, and the Mausoleum of Mohammed V bear witness to Morocco''s royal past and present. A walk through the Kasbah of the Udayas, with its white and blue Andalusian houses and peaceful gardens, feels like stepping into another world. The journey then continues north to Chefchaouen.</p>

    <p>This small mountain town, painted in shades of blue, invites you to wander its quiet streets. At sunset, the viewpoint at the Spanish Mosque offers a stunning view over the medina and the Rif Mountains. You spend the night in Chefchaouen.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation</strong>:<br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Casa Hassan or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Dar Echaouen or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 3: Chefchaouen - Volubilis - Meknes - Fes'
  html: '<p>Your third day begins with a drive from Chefchaouen through rolling hills to the Roman ruins of Volubilis. This UNESCO World Heritage site is filled with mosaics and columns that testify to the city''s past grandeur. A few kilometers away lies Meknes, once the capital under the reign of Sultan Moulay Ismail.</p>

    <p>Here, the great Bab Mansour Gate and the royal granaries reflect the sultan''s ambition. As evening approaches, the road leads you to Fes, the spiritual and cultural heart of Morocco, where you will spend the night.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation:</strong><br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Riad Fes Yamanda or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Palais Houyam or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 4: A Day to Visit Fes'
  html: '<p>The fourth day is entirely devoted to discovering Fes. The medieval medina of Fes el Bali is a maze of narrow streets steeped in history. You can visit the Bou Inania Madrasa, admire the oldest university in the world at Al-Qarawiyyin, and witness the colorful traditions of leather dyeing in the tanneries.</p>

    <p>The gates of the royal palace and the old Jewish quarter, the Mellah, offer a broader perspective on the city''s diverse heritage. As evening falls, Fes reveals itself to be a living museum of Morocco''s rich and complex past.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation:</strong><br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Riad Fes Yamanda or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Palais Houyam or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 5: Fes - Azrou - Ifrane - Midelt - Erfoud - Merzouga'
  html: '<p>On the fifth day, you will head south, crossing the Middle Atlas Mountains. The first stop is Ifrane, often nicknamed Morocco''s "little Switzerland" because of its European-style architecture. Further on lies Azrou, where cedar forests are home to Barbary macaques.</p>

    <p>You will have lunch in Midelt before the road descends into the Ziz Valley, lined with thousands of palm trees. After crossing Erfoud, famous for its fossils and dates, the journey continues until the golden dunes of Merzouga appear on the horizon. Here, a camel ride or a 4x4 transfer takes you into the heart of the Erg Chebbi desert. The evening unfolds under a starry sky in a desert camp.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation</strong>:<br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Lhomme du Desert or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Madu or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 6: Merzouga - Nomadic Families - Rocky Desert - Erg Chebbi Dunes'
  html: '<p>On the sixth day, the rhythm of desert life begins. You will meet nomadic families who continue to live in tents and practice traditions passed down through generations. The rocky desert landscapes surrounding Merzouga reveal mineral quarries and plateaus of black volcanic rock.</p>

    <p>In the village of Khamlia, the sounds of gnawa music echo a spiritual heritage from sub-Saharan Africa. Later, the dunes of Erg Chebbi invite you to climb, relax, or try sandboarding. As the sun sets on the horizon, a camel trek takes you further into the dunes for another unforgettable night in the desert.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation</strong>:<br/><em>Standard</em>: Mouhou Standard Camp or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Mouhou Luxury Camp or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 7: Merzouga - Tinghir - Todra Gorge - Valley of Roses - Ouarzazate'
  html: '<p>The seventh day is a return to civilization, but not without wonders along the way. Leaving Merzouga, you cross Rissani and Tinghir, where the Todra Gorge rises spectacularly, its cliffs reaching nearly 300 meters high.</p>

    <p>A walk through this canyon lets you admire the raw power of nature. Continuing on, the Dades Valley offers winding roads and spectacular rock formations before you cross the Valley of Roses, famous every spring for its fragrant flowers.</p>

    <p>In the evening, you arrive in Ouarzazate, often called the Hollywood of Morocco for its film studios and desert landscapes.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation:</strong><br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Riad Dar Barbara or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Dar Chamaa or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 8: Ouarzazate - Kasbah Ait Benhaddou - High Atlas - Marrakech'
  html: '<p>The eighth day''s adventure takes you to one of Morocco''s most iconic sites, the fortified village of Ait Benhaddou. This UNESCO World Heritage site has served as the backdrop for countless films and TV series, yet remains a living kasbah for some families.</p>

    <p>After exploring its maze of earthen buildings, the road climbs into the High Atlas Mountains. After crossing the Tizi n''Tichka pass, the highest in Morocco, you will descend toward Marrakech.</p>

    <p>As night falls, the famous Jemaa el-Fnaa square comes alive with food stalls, performers, and storytellers.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation:</strong><br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Riad Marana or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Bahia Salam or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 9: Day Dedicated to Visiting Marrakech'
  html: '<p>The 9th day is entirely dedicated to Marrakech. The Koutoubia Mosque stands proudly with its graceful minaret, while Bahia Palace enchants visitors with its intricate tilework and gardens. The Saadian Tombs tell the story of past dynasties, and the Majorelle Garden, once owned by Yves Saint Laurent, offers a peaceful escape in a city brimming with energy. Marrakech''s souks, meanwhile, buzz with artisans selling all kinds of goods, from spices to carpets.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation:</strong><br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Riad Marana or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Bahia Salam or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 10: Day Trip to Ouzoud'
  html: '<p>On the 10th day, you leave the city for a trip to the Ouzoud waterfalls, located about two and a half hours from Marrakech. These are the highest waterfalls in Morocco, plunging into emerald pools below.</p>

    <p>A short hike lets you discover stunning viewpoints, and along the way, you might spot mischievous Barbary macaques. Back in Marrakech in the evening, you will dive back into the rhythm of the city.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation:</strong><br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Riad Marana or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Bahia Salam or similar</p>'
- title: 'Day 11: Marrakech - Essaouira'
  html: '<p>The 11th day brings a change of scenery as you head west to Essaouira, on the Atlantic coast. With its blue-trimmed whitewashed houses, narrow streets, and sea breeze, Essaouira stands apart from every other city in Morocco.</p>

    <p>The medina, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is calm and charming, while the busy fishing port reveals fishermen at work. The Skala fortress offers a stunning view over the ocean, and the seafood here is some of the freshest you can enjoy. The day ends in this coastal haven.</p>

    <p><strong>Accommodation</strong>:<br/><em>Standard</em>: Hotel Riad Maison du Sud or similar<br/><em>Superior</em>: Hotel Riad Raoud Rayhane or similar</p>'
