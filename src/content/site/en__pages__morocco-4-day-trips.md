Even in four days, you can see and do a lot in Morocco. Start in **Marrakech** or **Fes**, two of Morocco’s famous cities. Walk through the busy streets, explore the markets, and see old palaces. You can try local food and enjoy music and street life.

A visit to a traditional hammam is a great way to relax. The steam, scrubs, and massage will help you feel refreshed after exploring the city.

Spend a day in the Atlas Mountains. Villages like Imlil are perfect for hiking and seeing Mount Toubkal, North Africa’s highest peak. You will enjoy nature and the warm hospitality of the Berber people.

If you have time, you can also visit a nearby valley or the Agafay Desert. Morocco’s landscapes are very different from the city, giving you a mix of adventure and calm.

In just four days, you can enjoy Morocco’s cities, culture, mountains, and deserts. It is a short trip but full of memories.

## **Imperial Cities**

From Casablanca, you could head inland towards the Imperial cities, such as Fes or Marrakech, each offering a unique glimpse into Morocco’s history. Marrakech, with its lively medina, is a sensory adventure. The narrow alleyways reveal hidden riads, artisan workshops, and bustling souks where spices, textiles, and handcrafted goods are sold. Jemaa el-Fnaa, the main square, comes alive at night with storytellers, musicians, and food stalls. Marrakech is also the gateway to the Atlas Mountains, where dramatic landscapes and traditional Berber villages await. A short drive into the mountains brings you to scenic valleys and snow-capped peaks in winter, offering a refreshing contrast to the city’s heat. The Atlas Mountains are perfect for short hikes, immersing visitors in nature while experiencing the warm hospitality of local Berber communities.

## **The Sahara Desert**

No trip to Morocco is complete without visiting the Sahara Desert. From Marrakech, a long drive or an organized tour can bring you to Merzouga, on the edge of the Erg Chebbi dunes. Experiencing the desert is magical, especially during sunrise or sunset when the golden sands shift dramatically with the light. Visitors can ride camels across the dunes, enjoy traditional Berber music by a campfire, and spend the night under a starlit sky in a desert camp. The desert is not just a natural wonder; it is a place to feel the silence and vastness of Morocco, creating unforgettable memories.

## **Tangier**

Heading north, the city of Tangier offers a completely different perspective. Located at the crossroads between the Mediterranean and the Atlantic, Tangier is a city of artistic and cultural fusion. Its medina, perched on a hill above the port, reflects centuries of diverse influences, from Arab and Berber to European colonial. Wandering through the narrow streets, travelers can discover historic palaces, bustling markets, and stunning viewpoints overlooking the Strait of Gibraltar. Tangier’s vibrant cafés and coastal scenery also provide a relaxing atmosphere, perfect for ending a busy exploration.

In four days, Morocco’s beauty can be experienced in layers: the modern energy of Casablanca, the historical richness of Marrakech or Fes, the natural majesty of the Atlas Mountains, the timeless allure of the Sahara Desert, and the cosmopolitan charm of Tangier. While the time is short, the journey offers an immersive glimpse into Morocco’s vibrant culture, diverse landscapes, and centuries-old traditions, leaving travelers with a deep appreciation for this remarkable country.
