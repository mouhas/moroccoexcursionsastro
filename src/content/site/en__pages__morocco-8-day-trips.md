## What are the best things to do in Morocco in 8 days?

Spending 8 days in Morocco allows you to enjoy diverse landscapes, from bustling cities like Casablanca to mountain towns like Chefchaouen, desert adventures in the Sahara, and relaxing beaches in Agadir.

Spending 8 days in Morocco gives you the chance to discover what Morocco has to offer, from the bustling streets of Casablanca to the blue hills of Chefchaouen. There’s something for every traveler.

## Modern City with History

Casablanca is Morocco’s largest city and a modern hub with historic charm. Visit the **Hassan II Mosque**, one of the largest in the world, and stroll along the **Corniche** for seaside views. The city also has art galleries, modern cafés, and markets to explore.

**Travel Tip:** Casablanca is more modern than other Moroccan cities, so expect wider streets and more contemporary restaurants and shops.

## Chefchaouen

Chefchaouen, in the Rif Mountains, is famous for its blue-painted streets and charming medina. It’s a perfect place for photography, relaxing walks, and exploring local handicrafts. Try shopping for woven blankets, pottery, or natural argan oil products.

**Travel Tip:** The town is hilly, so wear comfortable shoes for walking. Early mornings or late afternoons are the best times for photos with soft light.

## Royal History and Monuments

Meknes is a city rich in history, known for its grand gates, palaces, and historic medina. Visit **Bab Mansour**, the most famous city gate, and explore the royal stables and granaries. Meknes offers a quieter alternative to Fes while still giving a glimpse of Morocco’s imperial past.

**Travel Tip:** Meknes is very walkable, but hiring a local guide can make your visit more informative and enjoyable.

## Adventure in the Sahara

Visiting the Sahara Desert is a must in Morocco. Camel rides across the golden dunes, sandboarding, or 4×4 desert trips create unforgettable experiences. Spending a night in a desert camp under the stars is magical.

**Travel Tip:** Nights in the desert can be chilly, so bring warm clothes. During the day, sunscreen, hats, and sunglasses are essential.

## Morocco’s Beach Destination

Agadir offers sandy beaches, modern resorts, and a relaxed atmosphere. Surfing, swimming, or enjoying fresh seafood along the coast is ideal for a break from city sightseeing. The nearby **Sous Massa National Park** is great for nature walks and bird watching.

**Travel Tip:** Agadir is more tourist-friendly and easy to navigate, making it suitable for families or travelers who want a more relaxed pace.

## Explore the Atlas Mountains

The **Atlas Mountains** provide stunning scenery and outdoor activities like hiking, trekking, and visiting Berber villages. Ifrane, sometimes called “Little Switzerland,” is a peaceful mountain town with beautiful parks and alpine-style architecture.

**Travel Tip:** Even in summer, mornings and evenings in the mountains can be cool, so bring layers and comfortable walking shoes.

## Moroccan Cuisine

Moroccan food is rich and flavorful. Enjoy **tagine**, **couscous**, **pastilla**, and street food like **msemen** and **sfenj**. Drinking **mint tea** is a central part of local hospitality.

**Travel Tip:** Eat at popular local spots for freshness and quality, and always wash hands before eating street food.

## Explore Local Markets

Moroccan markets are full of life, colors, and smells. You can buy spices, rugs, leather goods, and lanterns. Haggling is common, so enjoy the experience and interact with local artisans.

**Travel Tip:** Carry small change and a backpack or small bag for easier movement through crowded markets.

## General Travel Tips for 8 Days in Morocco

1. **Dress Modestly:** Cover shoulders and knees, especially in rural areas or near religious sites.
2. **Stay Hydrated:** Bring water, especially in desert and inland regions.
3. **Transport:** Trains and buses are common for city travel; private transfers or 4×4 vehicles are better for remote areas.
4. **Respect Local Customs:** Always ask before photographing people, and be mindful of local traditions.
5. **Currency:** Moroccan dirham (MAD) is used; carry some cash, as many places don’t accept cards.
