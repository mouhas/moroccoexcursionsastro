    aHtml: '<p>No. Most travelers pay a 10% deposit to secure their dates and settle the rest in cash (or by card, depending on the tour) with the driver-guide at the start of the trip. If you prefer, you can also pay in full online at the time of booking.</p>'
  - q: What's the difference between a private and a group tour?
    aHtml: '<p>Private tours use your own vehicle and driver-guide, with an itinerary built around your pace and interests — you don''t share the trip with strangers. Group (shared) tours join you with other travelers in a minibus on a fixed itinerary and are priced per person, which makes them more affordable for solo travelers or small budgets.</p>'
  - q: Can I customize a private tour itinerary?
    aHtml: '<p>Yes, almost every private tour on this site can be adjusted — added days, different start/end cities, extra stops, or an upgraded desert camp and hotels. Tell us what you have in mind when you send a booking request and we''ll adapt the route around it.</p>'
  - q: What's the best time of year to visit Morocco?
    aHtml: '<p>Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are the most comfortable for both the cities and the Sahara. Summer (June–August) is very hot in the desert, especially in Merzouga and Zagora, while winter brings cold nights in the desert and snow in the Atlas Mountains — all manageable with the right layers, but worth planning for.</p>'
  - q: How physically demanding are the desert tours?
    aHtml: '<p>Most of our tours are easy to moderate — mainly time in the vehicle with short walks, a camel trek at a gentle pace, and optional activities like sandboarding or a hike in the Todra or Dades gorges. None require technical climbing or advanced fitness, though multi-day tours do involve several hours of driving per day.</p>'
  - q: What should I pack for a Morocco desert tour?
    aHtml: '<p>Layers are the key word: light, breathable clothing for the day, a warm jacket for cold desert nights, comfortable closed shoes, a scarf or hat for sun and wind, sunscreen, and a headlamp or flashlight for the camp. A power bank is useful since charging in the desert camp is limited.</p>'
  - q: What's included in the desert camp accommodation?
    aHtml: '<p>Standard camps have shared or private tents with proper beds and bedding, a shared dinner around the campfire with Berber music, and basic toilet facilities. Superior/luxury camps add en-suite tents with private bathrooms and a more elevated dinner service — both options are shown on each tour''s pricing table.</p>'
  - q: Is it safe to travel in Morocco, including the desert regions?
    aHtml: '<p>Yes, Morocco is a well-established destination for international travelers, and our routes through the Atlas Mountains and Sahara are ones we run regularly with experienced local drivers who know the roads and the desert well. As with any trip, keep the usual common-sense precautions for valuables and follow your guide''s advice in remote areas.</p>'
  - q: How many people are typically in a private tour vehicle?
    aHtml: '<p>Private tours are exclusive to your own group — just you, your travel companions, and the driver-guide. Vehicle size (sedan, minivan, or larger van) is matched to your group size, from solo travelers up to larger families or groups of friends.</p>'
  - q: What languages do your guides speak?
    aHtml: '<p>Our driver-guides are multilingual, most comfortably in English and French, with several also speaking Spanish, Italian, or German. Let us know your preferred language when booking and we''ll match you with a guide who speaks it.</p>'
  - q: Is tipping expected for drivers and guides?
    aHtml: '<p>Tipping isn''t mandatory but is appreciated and customary in Morocco for good service — most travelers tip their driver-guide directly at the end of the trip. There''s no fixed amount; it''s entirely at your discretion based on your experience.</p>'
  - q: Will I have phone or internet signal in the desert?
    aHtml: '<p>Coverage is generally reliable in towns and along most routes, including Merzouga and Zagora, though it can drop out in remote stretches of the Atlas Mountains or right in the dunes themselves. Most desert camps do not have Wi-Fi, which is part of what makes the night under the stars feel disconnected in a good way.</p>'
  - q: Can you arrange airport pickup and drop-off?
    aHtml: '<p>Yes, pickup and drop-off from your hotel or the airport in your tour''s start/end city is included in the private tour price — just share your flight details or accommodation when you book so we can plan the timing.</p>'
  - q: Are your tours suitable for families with children?
    aHtml: '<p>Yes, private tours in particular are easy to adapt for families — the pace, stops, and activities (like the camel trek) can be adjusted for younger children, and vehicles are chosen to fit the whole family comfortably. Let us know the ages of any children traveling when you book.</p>'
